Privacy policy

Last updated: 23 October 2026

(“the app”) estimates a physique score and a body-fat range from two photos, then gives you a weekly plan and check-in. This page explains what it collects, where that goes, how long it’s kept and what you can do about it.

The short version

  • Your photos stay on your phone. Our server never stores them.
  • Your face is blurred on the phone before a photo leaves it.
  • To score a check-in, the blurred photos go to a third-party AI model, which may keep them for up to 30 days, longer only if they’re flagged for a safety review.
  • We don’t ask for your name or email address.
  • You can export your data or delete your account in the app’s Settings at any time.
  • We don’t sell your data and we don’t use it for advertising.

Not a medical device. The app gives a visual estimate from photos. It isn’t made to detect, treat or monitor any medical condition. Talk to a doctor before changing your diet or training.

1. Who we are

The app is made and sold by Plucore (“we”, “us”). We decide how your data is used, which makes us the “controller” under UK and EU data protection law.

Contact: support@plucore.com

2. Your photos

  • Where they live: in a private vault inside the app on your phone. The vault is left out of iCloud and computer backups, and Face ID guards it. They stay there until you delete them or your account.
  • Before anything leaves your phone: the app finds your face on the phone (with Apple’s built-in tools) and blurs it, removes location and other hidden details from the file, and shrinks the image to 1000 × 1333 pixels.
  • When they leave: only after you’ve subscribed and agreed to send them for scoring. Nothing goes to the AI before you pay.
  • What happens to them: our server passes the blurred photos to a third-party AI model, which reads them 3 times to steady the result. Along with the photos it gets only your sex and whether you’re an adult or a teen, never your date of birth or anything that names you. The server holds the photos in memory for that one call and stores none of them. They’re never written to our logs.
  • What the AI provider keeps: it may keep the photos for up to 30 days, longer only if they’re flagged for a safety review.
  • A fingerprint, not a copy: our server keeps a one-way fingerprint (a “hash”) of each photo so the same photos always get the same answer. A photo can’t be rebuilt from it.

4. What stays on your phone

Your photos, a copy of your quiz answers, your settings, and any log entries made offline (until they’re sent). These are deleted when you delete your account in the app. Deleting the app also deletes them, but it doesn’t delete your account on our server: use Settings → Delete my account first.

5. Usage analytics

We use PostHog, hosted in the EU (Frankfurt), to see where people get stuck in the app. It receives only events from a fixed list:

  • which onboarding step you reached or finished (the step’s name, not your answer);
  • consent choices: given, declined or withdrawn;
  • the subscription screen: seen or closed, which of two price groups you were shown, and whether a purchase or restore went through;
  • photo steps: which angle (front, side, back) and whether the photo passed the phone’s checks;
  • the scan’s outcome type (for example “scored” or “not scored”), never the score;
  • which tab you opened.

It never gets your health answers, scores, ranges, photos, dates or anything you type. Events are linked to your random account ID, not to your name. PostHog’s software adds standard device details such as the phone model, iOS version and app version. Your IP address is discarded.

Adults: on unless you turn it off. Teens (13–17): off, and we never ask you to turn it on. Nothing is sent before you’ve entered your date of birth. Switch it any time in Settings → Share usage data. Deleting your account deletes your analytics record and events too (PostHog can take a few days to finish).

6. Crash reports

We use Sentry to find and fix crashes. A report holds the error type, an error code, where in the code it happened, and the phone model, iOS version and app version. It never holds photos, health answers, error messages, screenshots or your account ID, and we’ve asked Sentry not to store IP addresses. Reports are kept only as long as needed to fix the problem.

7. Purchases

Subscriptions are sold and billed by Apple. We never see your card details or your Apple ID.

We use RevenueCat to manage subscriptions. It receives your purchase history from Apple (which subscription, renewals, trials, cancellations) linked to your random account ID. It gets no name, email or health data. When you delete your account, we delete your record at RevenueCat. Apple keeps its own purchase records under Apple’s privacy policy.

8. Apple Health

If you allow it, the app reads your latest weight from Apple Health when you ask it to, and only that. It never writes anything to Apple Health. Data from Apple Health is never used for advertising or sold.

9. Sign in with Apple

After you subscribe, you can save your progress with Sign in with Apple, so you can get it back on a new phone. We don’t ask Apple for your name or email address. Apple gives us an ID for you that only this app uses. We also keep a token from Apple, never shown to the app, so we can cancel the link with Apple when you delete your account.

10. Why we’re allowed to use it

UK and EU data protection law (the GDPR) needs a legal reason, called a “lawful basis”, for each use. Ours, in plain words:

Send your blurred photos to the third-party AI model for scoringYour explicit consent, on its own screen, withdrawable in Settings.
Run your account and the subscription you bought, restore purchases, export and delete your dataContract: we need it to give you what you paid for.
Usage analytics (adults)Legitimate interests: understanding where the app gets in people’s way. You can turn it off at any time.
Crash reports, the daily scan limit, keeping the service secureLegitimate interests: keeping the app working and safe.

Withdrawing consent stops the use from then on. It doesn’t make earlier use unlawful. Without both consents the app can’t score a check-in.

11. Where your data is processed

  • Our database and server: Supabase, in Ireland (EU).
  • Analytics: PostHog, in Frankfurt, Germany (EU).
  • Crash reports: Sentry, in Germany (EU).
  • The third-party AI model: its provider’s servers, which may be in the United States.
  • Subscriptions: RevenueCat, in the United States; and Apple.

When data goes to a country without UK or EU “adequacy” status, we rely on the safeguards the law allows, such as the standard contractual clauses in our providers’ terms. Ask us for details at support@plucore.com.

12. How long we keep it

  • Photos: on your phone until you delete them. Never on our server. The AI provider: up to 30 days, longer only if flagged for a safety review.
  • Everything on our server: until you delete it or your account. Quiz answers go sooner if you withdraw your health-data consent.
  • After you delete your account: it’s removed from our database straight away, and from backups within 30 days. Your analytics record and your RevenueCat record are deleted at the same time.
  • Crash reports: only as long as needed to fix the problem, within Sentry’s own retention limits.

13. Your rights

Wherever you live, you can:

  • Get a copy of your data: Settings → Export my data gives you a file with your account, consents, scores, ranges and logs, plus the answers kept on your phone. Your photos export separately, from Settings → Your photos. For anything else we hold, such as the scoring records in section 3, email us.
  • Delete it: Settings → Delete my account. This doesn’t cancel an App Store subscription; cancel that in the Settings app first (see how).
  • Withdraw consent: Settings → Privacy, for health data and for AI scoring.
  • Turn off analytics: Settings → Share usage data.
  • Correct it: change your answers in the app, or email us.
  • Object to or restrict how we use it, or move it to another service: email us.

Email support@plucore.com. Because we don’t know your name or email, we may ask for your account ID (shown in your data export) to find your data. We reply within one month.

You can also complain to a data protection regulator: in the UK, the Information Commissioner’s Office; in the EU or EEA, the regulator where you live. We’d like the chance to put it right first.

14. Age rules and teens

  • You must be at least 13 to use the app, or at least 16 in the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway. The app works out your region from your App Store country, or your phone’s region if that isn’t known; if neither is known, the minimum is 16.
  • The age check uses your month and year of birth. Under the minimum age, the app stops before any account is made.
  • 13 to 17 year olds use teen mode: analytics off by default and never nudged on; no “your scan is ready” reminders; no share card; the AI is told only “teen”, never a birth date, and follows extra child-safety rules.
  • For everyone: photos must be of yourself, in shorts or gym clothes. The scan refuses nudity.
  • If we learn an account belongs to someone under the minimum age, we delete it. Parents or guardians can contact us at support@plucore.com.

15. Security

Data travels to our server encrypted (HTTPS). Database rules let each account read only its own data, and only our server can write scores and subscription status. On the phone, the photo vault sits behind Face ID and is left out of backups. No system is perfectly secure, but we keep what we hold small: no photos, no names, no email addresses.

16. This website

This site uses no cookies, analytics or trackers. It’s hosted by GitHub Pages, which may log your IP address for security. The fonts load from Google Fonts, so Google receives your IP address when you open a page.
